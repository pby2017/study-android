# Card refinement audit — MobileLLM-Flash

Time: 2026-11-22 04:38 KST

## Sources checked
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15954
- https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15954v2
- Exact-name GitHub searches for `MobileLLM-Flash` and `2603.15954`; results surfaced the older 2024 `facebookresearch/MobileLLM`, not a verified Flash artifact. Did not attribute its code or 1.5k stars to Flash.

## Fact check and changes
- Reframed the card from a product/implementation claim to an engineering lesson: benchmark target-device latency and quality, not parameter count or FLOPs alone.
- Experiment details in the paper: candidate models exported with ExecuTorch 1.1; Galaxy S25; XNNPACK mobile CPU; 4-bit weights (group 32), 8-bit dynamic activations, quantized KV cache; 4 CPU threads; contexts 1k/2k/4k; warmup and three-run average.
- The paper reports up to 1.8x prefill and 1.6x decode versus LFM2 at the tested scale; result is scoped to the paper's hardware/runtime/quantization benchmark, not generalized to Android devices.
- The 35% figure is approximately the continued-pretraining tokens compared with MobileLLM-Pro's pretraining. The full pipeline additionally uses 200 x 2.6B candidate-search tokens and 800B instruction-tuning tokens per final model; the old card omitted these material qualifications.
- Shallow-and-wide is not universally optimal: the paper describes a quality/latency trade-off. It favors interleaved attention skipping in its search and reports degradation when more than three skip-attention layers are consecutive.
- The paper says standard operators are ExecuTorch-compatible. Exact-name search did not verify a publicly released MobileLLM-Flash code/model repository; compatibility in the paper is not evidence that a ready-to-use Android SDK/model package is published.
- Attention signal: a recent technical paper with detailed Galaxy S25 and iPhone 17 measurements; no star count claimed. The popular Meta MobileLLM GitHub repository is a separate older project, so its star count was intentionally excluded.

## Decision
Retain as an AI+Android engineering lesson; remove “Meta product/framework”, “35% tokens to equal quality”, and unqualified ExecuTorch-compatibility framing. Add experiment conditions and an interview answer that distinguishes continued pretraining from the entire training/search cost.

Confidence: high for claims reported by the paper and benchmark setup; no independent replication or public Flash artifact was verified.
